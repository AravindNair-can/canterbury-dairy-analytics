# Canterbury Dairy Farm Operational Analytics

## Project Overview
This project models and analyses the operational performance of a 741-cow, spring-calving dairy farm in Canterbury, New Zealand.

The goal is to build a farm-level analytics system that helps explain production variability, cost efficiency, and operational risk using realistic, decision-focused data analysis. The project is designed to evolve incrementally into dashboards and a complete analytics pipeline, mirroring how real-world analytics systems are built over time.

---

## Business Context
Dairy farming operations in Canterbury are highly sensitive to seasonal patterns, weather variability, feed strategy, and animal health issues such as lameness.

This project is grounded in real on-farm operational exposure, including:
- spring calving patterns occurring between August and October, with early November tail-end calving,
- associated production ramp-up and peak during early to mid-lactation,
- the impact of unpredictable rainfall during summer months,
- feed transitions from silage in winter and spring to pasture-based systems,
- irrigation usage during dry periods,
- and production losses associated with lameness events.

The analytics focus is not theoretical optimisation, but practical insight generation that could support farm-level decision-making.

---


## Current Scope and Roadmap
**Current scope:**
- Farm-level production modelling
- Weather and lameness impact analysis
- Cost and efficiency KPIs
- Clear documentation and reproducible structure

**Planned evolution:**
- Interactive BI dashboards for operational monitoring
- Data validation and transformation layers
- Simulated ETL pipeline and database integration
- Scenario and forecasting extensions

This incremental approach reflects how analytics systems are typically developed in professional environments.

---

## Data Ethics and Transparency
The data used in this project is synthetically generated but grounded in realistic operational patterns, regional production benchmarks, and direct observational farm knowledge.

No proprietary, confidential, or real farm records are used. Assumptions and modelling choices are documented explicitly to maintain transparency and analytical integrity.
